Your RL run isn't learning. syntropyrl tells you why.

RL post-training rarely crashes. It just quietly fails to learn, and you find out eleven hours later. syntropyrl watches your run and names the failure mode in plain English: the logprob mismatch between your trainer and your generator, the phrase your grader secretly loves, the entropy that died at step 240.

There are sixteen ways to train a policy and no way to debug one

      
The RL tooling ecosystem grew fast in one direction. Trainers, environments, and
        async rollout engines are abundant. The layer that tells you why a run is failing
        was never built.

    

    

      

        
📦

        
Trainers assume you're fine

        
TRL, verl, OpenRLHF, and a dozen others will happily train a policy against a
          broken advantage signal for a week. They log the metrics. Interpreting them is
          left as an exercise.

      

      

        
📊

        
Trackers are RL-blind

        
W&B and TensorBoard draw beautiful lines. They have no opinion about the fact
          that 78% of your GRPO groups have zero advantage, which is the only thing that
          matters on that chart.

      

      

        
💬

        
The knowledge lives in Discord

        
The people who know these failure modes learned them at 2am. That knowledge sits
          in scattered threads and one excellent blog post. It has never been written down
          as something you can run.

      

    

  







  

    

      
Three lines, then read the diagnosis

      
syntropyrl does not wrap your trainer, patch your model, or own your loop. You hand it
        the metrics you already log, and it hands back a diagnosis.

    

    

      

        
1

        
Instrument

        
One object. No config file, no server, no account.


from syntropyrl import Doctor

doctor = Doctor()

      

      

        
2

        
Observe

        
Pass whatever you have. Missing fields just disable the detectors that need them.


for i, batch in enumerate(trainer.train()):
    doctor.observe(
        step=i,
        rollouts=batch.rollouts,  # optional
        **batch.metrics,          # what you log
    )

      

      

        
3

        
Read

        
Plain English, with the numbers that justify it and a fix to try first.


print(doctor.report())

# or fail loudly in CI:
Doctor(raise_on_critical=True)

      

    

  







  

    

      
The expensive one

      
Your trainer and your generator disagree about the same tokens

      
Inference engines compute logprobs with different
        kernels than your training forward pass. Chunked prefill, paged attention, and parallel
        reductions all shift the numbers slightly. Your importance ratios are then computed against
        logprobs the trainer never produced.

      
Nothing crashes. Loss curves look normal.
        The policy gradient is simply wrong, and you find out after the run.
        syntropyrl measures the gap every step, and correlates it with sequence length to tell a
        kernel difference apart from a prompt-boundary bug.

      Read RLD-014 in the Atlas →
    

    


# Pre-flight, before you spend money
from syntropyrl import (
    check_logprob_parity, parity_report,
)

result = check_logprob_parity(
    generator_logprobs=vllm_scores,   # callable
    trainer_logprobs=hf_scores,       # callable
    samples=probe_texts,              # incl. a long one
)
print(parity_report(result))
assert result["verdict"] == "pass", result["code"]

# verdict           : FAIL  RLD-014
# mean gap          : 0.0081200
# corr(gap, length) : 0.71
# likely causes     :
#   chunked prefill or paged-attention
#   boundary (gap grows with length)

The Failure Atlas is the real project

      
Detectors answer "is this happening?". The Atlas answers "what is it, why does it happen,
        and what do I do?" - one page per failure mode, written for someone nine hours into
        debugging. The detector registry and the Atlas are generated from the same source, so the
        docs cannot drift from the code.

Five decisions this project will not revisit

      
syntropyrl runs inside other people's training images, at hour nine of a run they cannot
        restart. That constrains everything.

    

    

      

        
✓

        
Zero dependencies, forever

        
Pure standard library. It will never fight your torch, numpy, or transformers pin. CI
          asserts the dependency list is empty.

      

      

        
🛡

        
A broken detector can't kill your run

        
Every detector is called inside a guard. If one raises, it is recorded and skipped. A
          diagnostic tool that crashes training is worse than no tool.

      

      

        
🧭

        
Partial instrumentation works

        
Every detector declares what it needs. Give it only reward_mean and you get
          the reward detectors. Nothing errors because a field is missing.

      

      

        
🔐

        
No monkeypatching

        
No import hooks, no patched trainers, no magic. You call one method in your loop and you
          can read exactly what it does.

      

      

        
🤔

        
Honest about uncertainty

        
Detectors stay silent unless confident, and a clean report says so explicitly: no
          known failure mode fired, which is not the same as a good run.

      

      

        
📈

        
Numbers, not vibes

        
"Entropy collapsed" is useless. "Entropy fell 1.42 → 0.11 and 78% of groups now have
          zero advantage" is a lead you can act on.

      

    

  







  

    

      
Integrations

      
Bring your own trainer

      
Frameworks disagree about metric names.
        kl, objective/kl, actor/kl_loss, and
        policy/approx_kl are the same quantity. syntropyrl ships an alias table so you can
        pass a raw metrics dict from any of them and the right detectors light up.

      
There is a drop-in TRL callback, an alias-based adapter that
        covers verl and OpenRLHF, and a JSONL reader for runs that already finished.

    

    


# TRL: one callback
from syntropyrl.integrations import trl_callback

trainer = GRPOTrainer(..., callbacks=[trl_callback()])

# verl / OpenRLHF / any metrics dict
from syntropyrl.integrations import from_metrics

doctor.observe(step=i, **from_metrics(raw))

# or a run that already finished
$ syntropyrl diagnose run.jsonl
